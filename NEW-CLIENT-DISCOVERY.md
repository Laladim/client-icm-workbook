# New-client discovery workbook

Use SETUP.md for guided onboarding. Read supplied documents first, ask at most three missing questions at a time, and preserve supplied facts, evidence, interpretations and decisions. Unknown and not-applicable need a reason and next source. Never paste credentials.

Canonical source: `_shared/questions.json`. `FILE-BUILD-MAP.csv` maps every answer to its file.

## Engagement, outcomes and scope

Folder: `company/`. Core onboarding.

- **company.description** (core): What does this client do, for whom, and in which markets?
  Why: Establish the business before discussing formats. File: `company/engagement.md`.
- **engagement.contact** (core): Who is the day-to-day client contact and who can make decisions?
  Why: Avoid unclear delegation and repeated approval requests. File: `company/engagement.md`.
- **engagement.services** (core): Which digital-marketing services are in scope, and which are excluded?
  Why: Define the actual job and avoid unpriced work. File: `company/engagement.md`.
- **engagement.success** (core): What business outcome matters most, what is its baseline, and what would success look like?
  Why: Connect content to a meaningful business result. File: `company/engagement.md`.
- **engagement.deadlines** (core): What launch dates, seasonal events, milestones and dependencies matter?
  Why: Make delivery and campaign planning realistic. File: `company/engagement.md`.
- **engagement.budget**: What budget or time capacity is available for content, tools, production and promotion?
  Why: Keep the plan feasible; record confidential figures privately. File: `company/engagement.md`.
- **engagement.deliverables** (core): What deliverables, turnaround, revision limits and acceptance criteria were agreed?
  Why: Make the service agreement operational. File: `company/engagement.md`.
- **engagement.approver** (core): Who approves the onboarding brief and workspace?
  Why: Name a real human for decisions the agent cannot make. File: `company/engagement.md`.
- **engagement.timezone** (core): What timezone, working days, meeting rhythm and response times should we use?
  Why: Prevent scheduling and expectation errors. File: `company/engagement.md`.
- **engagement.escalation**: Who handles urgent incidents, corrections, complaints and approval delays?
  Why: Give uncertain or urgent work a safe next step. File: `company/engagement.md`.

## Company knowledge and operating responsibilities

Folder: `company/`. Core onboarding.

- **company.onboarding** (core): Which company overview, onboarding deck, service agreement and business SOPs already exist?
  Why: Reference current records before creating replacements. File: `company/operations.md`.
- **company.people** (core): Who does what across leadership, sales, product, support, marketing, design and technical work?
  Why: Build a delegation and handoff map. File: `company/operations.md`.
- **company.customers** (core): Where is the existing client/customer list and what profile fields can marketing access?
  Why: Learn segments while respecting privacy and permissions. File: `company/operations.md`.
- **company.accounts** (core): Which website, social accounts and listings are official, and who owns each?
  Why: Prevent work on an abandoned or unofficial account. File: `company/operations.md`.
- **company.workflows** (core): Which existing workflows or agencies already own this work? List their entry files or procedures if known.
  Why: Bind the current owner rather than creating a competing process. File: `company/operations.md`.
- **company.handover**: What knowledge is undocumented, who can cover each role, and where do accepted changes get recorded?
  Why: Make the workspace useful for another team member. File: `company/operations.md`.

## Brand strategy and target audience

Folder: `brand/`. Core onboarding.

- **brand.purpose** (core): Why does this business exist, beyond its products or services?
  Why: Anchor the internal brand. File: `brand/strategy/internal-brand.md`.
- **brand.vision**: What future is the business working toward, and which parts are aspirations?
  Why: Keep future ambition separate from current capabilities. File: `brand/strategy/internal-brand.md`.
- **brand.mission**: What does the business commit to doing now?
  Why: Describe the present mission. File: `brand/strategy/internal-brand.md`.
- **brand.values**: Which values should shape behavior, and what evidence shows them?
  Why: Keep values concrete without inventing outcomes. File: `brand/strategy/internal-brand.md`.
- **audience.primary** (core): Who is the primary audience, buyer, user and decision maker?
  Why: Choose whom the work serves; do not guess from an example. File: `brand/strategy/positioning.md`.
- **audience.jobs** (core): What situation makes them seek help, what job are they trying to finish, and what blocks it?
  Why: Find recognizable buying triggers and useful content. File: `brand/strategy/positioning.md`.
- **audience.research** (core): What do we know from customer research, and which fears, beliefs or segments are still hypotheses?
  Why: Separate researched evidence from assumptions. File: `brand/strategy/positioning.md`.
- **audience.exclusions**: Who is a poor fit, and which markets, claims or use cases are out of scope?
  Why: Avoid attracting the wrong demand. File: `brand/strategy/positioning.md`.
- **brand.competitors**: Which alternatives do customers consider, including doing nothing or doing it in-house?
  Why: Frame competitive research without assuming weaknesses. File: `brand/strategy/positioning.md`.
- **brand.difference** (core): What difference can the client prove, and what should the audience remember?
  Why: Ground positioning in evidence. File: `brand/strategy/positioning.md`.

## Voice, message and visual identity

Folder: `brand/`. Core onboarding.

- **brand.character**: What personality or archetype fits the brand, and how should it behave?
  Why: Translate character into writing choices without forcing a framework. File: `brand/identity/character.md`.
- **brand.voice** (core): How should the brand sound? Give accepted and rejected examples, terminology and style rules.
  Why: Make voice reproducible. File: `brand/identity/character.md`.
- **brand.messages** (core): Which offer wording, core messages, promise, stories, name and tagline are already approved?
  Why: Preserve actual approvals and leave undecided blocks open. File: `brand/identity/message.md`.
- **brand.claims** (core): Which phrases, comparisons, guarantees or claims need evidence or special review?
  Why: Avoid making unapproved promises. File: `brand/identity/message.md`.
- **brand.visuals** (core): Where are current logos, colors, fonts, photography rules, templates and asset rights?
  Why: Use the right design files without inventing a visual identity. File: `brand/identity/visual.md`.
- **brand.languages**: Which languages, reading level, accessibility and localization requirements apply?
  Why: Make the content usable by the intended audience. File: `brand/identity/character.md`.

## Before, during and after the sale

Folder: `brand/`. Core onboarding.

- **journey.before** (core): How do new prospects find the business, become engaged, and recognize a need?
  Why: Describe discovery and acquisition without confusing traffic with demand. File: `brand/marketing/before.md`.
- **journey.capture** (core): What action captures a lead or starts a purchase, and what value is offered?
  Why: Connect discovery to the next useful step. File: `brand/marketing/during.md`.
- **journey.nurture**: What happens between first interest and purchase: follow-up, objections, nurture and handoffs?
  Why: Plan support for real sales friction. File: `brand/marketing/during.md`.
- **journey.conversion** (core): What is the conversion path, who owns it, and what defines a qualified lead or sale?
  Why: Define the actual commercial event. File: `brand/marketing/during.md`.
- **journey.after** (core): How are customers onboarded, supported, retained, expanded or invited to refer?
  Why: Include customer experience, lifetime value and referrals. File: `brand/marketing/after.md`.
- **journey.content**: What content is needed at each step, what CTA fits, and what should never be pushed prematurely?
  Why: Map content to the journey rather than copying one format everywhere. File: `brand/marketing/during.md`.

## Customer evidence and proof permissions

Folder: `customer-evidence/`. Core onboarding.

- **evidence.calls** (core): Where are demo, discovery or sales calls, interviews, support questions and loss reasons?
  Why: Find the actual language and questions behind stronger content. File: `customer-evidence/proof.md`.
- **evidence.profiles**: Which customer profiles, use cases and buying-group details are current?
  Why: Avoid mixing different audiences or prospect and customer states. File: `customer-evidence/proof.md`.
- **evidence.case-studies** (core): Which case studies exist, what was the result, and where are the source and client approval?
  Why: Keep exact evidence and publication consent connected. File: `customer-evidence/proof.md`.
- **evidence.testimonials** (core): Which testimonials and one-line praises may be quoted, named or paraphrased, and under what permission?
  Why: A proof format is not permission to invent or shorten a quote. File: `customer-evidence/proof.md`.
- **evidence.results**: Which metrics, before/after claims or screenshots have reproducible evidence and an approved time window?
  Why: Do not combine unrelated results or turn estimates into outcomes. File: `customer-evidence/proof.md`.
- **evidence.gaps**: What proof is missing, who can supply it, and which public uses are blocked until then?
  Why: Create an evidence request instead of filling gaps with plausible stories. File: `customer-evidence/proof.md`.
- **evidence.libraries** (core): Which VOC/customer-value themes, demo-call-flow, industry/news, competitor and coverage research libraries exist, and who owns their accepted outputs?
  Why: Route to rich research and proof stores, not only a product-facts checker. File: `customer-evidence/research-libraries.md`.
- **evidence.earned-media**: Where is the record of backlinks, PR mentions, citations and peer shares, including date, exact URL, relevance, owner and any outreach limits?
  Why: Observed links and mentions are evidence; a prospect list is not an earned placement. File: `customer-evidence/earned-media.md`.

## Products, services and release communication

Folder: `offers/`. Core onboarding.

- **offers.main** (core): What products or services are being marketed, what do they do today, and what are their limitations?
  Why: Works for products, services, local businesses and nonprofit programs. File: `offers/catalog.md`.
- **offers.sources** (core): Who owns product/service truth, and where are current API docs when relevant, help center, FAQs, release notes and approved offer details?
  Why: Marketing copy is a consumer of product truth, not its proof. File: `offers/catalog.md`.
- **offers.changes** (core): How will we learn about releases, policy changes, discontinued offers, incidents or corrections?
  Why: Prevent important business updates from remaining internal. File: `offers/catalog.md`.
- **offers.surfaces** (core): Which online and sales surfaces need an update for each change, and who owns them?
  Why: Track documentation, website, email, social, sales and existing content coverage. File: `offers/catalog.md`.
- **offers.pricing**: May prices, discounts, guarantees or competitor costs appear publicly? What approval is needed?
  Why: Public pricing policies differ by client. File: `offers/catalog.md`.
- **offers.launches**: What embargoes, launch readiness checks, rollback conditions or announcement sequence apply?
  Why: An internal plan or feature flag is not proof of a released capability. File: `offers/catalog.md`.

## Channel scope and platform strategy

Folder: `channels/`. Core onboarding.

- **channels.active** (core): Which channels are actually in scope? Use channel IDs from the list; an empty list means no channel production.
  Why: Enable relevant branches rather than assuming every client needs every platform. File: `channels/strategy.md`.
- **channels.roles** (core): For each selected channel, what audience, job, format, CTA and conversion does it serve?
  Why: Give each platform a deliberate role. File: `channels/strategy.md`.
- **channels.cadence** (core): What cadence and production capacity are realistic, and who creates each asset?
  Why: Avoid promising volume without people or inputs. File: `channels/strategy.md`.
- **channels.inventory** (core): Where are existing blog, newsletter, YouTube, LinkedIn and other selected-channel assets, inventories, editorial calendars and performance exports?
  Why: Start from available material before making more. File: `channels/strategy.md`.
- **channels.repurpose**: Which source assets may be repurposed, who owns the original, and which derivatives are required or optional?
  Why: Preserve source identity and avoid duplicating ownership. File: `channels/strategy.md`.
- **channels.reviewers** (core): Who reviews each selected channel for audience fit, factual accuracy and final acceptance, and what happens when a reviewer is missing?
  Why: A new audience must reach the actual worker and reviewer; never silently fall back to an old persona. File: `channels/strategy.md`.

## Measurement, sales feedback and reporting

Folder: `performance/`. Core onboarding.

- **performance.kpis** (core): Which business outcomes, leading indicators and quality measures will be reported?
  Why: Separate traffic, engagement, qualified demand, sales and retention. File: `performance/measurement.md`.
- **performance.sources** (core): Where are website analytics (for example GA4), search-console data (for example Google Search Console), CRM, sales-stage history and channel exports? Who owns definitions, access and refresh?
  Why: Register actual sources rather than plausible connectors. File: `performance/measurement.md`.
- **performance.attribution**: How are conversions defined, deduplicated and attributed, and what cannot be proven?
  Why: Do not claim content caused revenue from correlation alone. File: `performance/measurement.md`.
- **performance.windows** (core): What reporting windows, baselines, lag and comparison rules apply?
  Why: Avoid misleading period comparisons. File: `performance/measurement.md`.
- **performance.feedback**: How will sales and customer success report lead quality, objections, wins and losses?
  Why: Close the loop from content to commercial learning. File: `performance/measurement.md`.
- **performance.reporting** (core): Who receives which report, how often, and what decision should follow?
  Why: Make reporting useful rather than another dashboard. File: `performance/measurement.md`.
- **performance.recurring-reports** (core): Which recurring status reports and private meeting briefs are required, for whom, with what privacy, source, owner and review gate?
  Why: A sales/conversion dashboard does not replace a written client report or meeting brief. File: `performance/reporting.md`.
- **performance.dictionary** (core): Define the metrics we will compare across channels, including units, denominators, attribution, timezone, windows, lag and source-specific limits.
  Why: Views, opens, clicks, qualified leads and sales need explicit definitions before cross-channel study. File: `performance/metrics-dictionary.md`.
- **performance.studies**: What bounded cross-channel question should we study, for which dates and cohort, and which owner outputs can support it?
  Why: Create a small cited evidence pack rather than searching every client file or copying a data warehouse. File: `performance/cross-channel-studies.md`.
- **performance.keywords**: Where is the keyword bank, with query intent, audience, geography, volume/rank source, observed date and page/topic mapping?
  Why: Keep measured query data separate from keyword ideas. File: `performance/search-and-ai.md`.
- **performance.ai-answers**: Where is the ChatGPT/AI-answer prompt and answer bank, with exact prompt, model/product, run date, locale, answer, cited URLs and repeat-check rule?
  Why: One model answer is an observation under stated conditions, not universal visibility. File: `performance/search-and-ai.md`.

## Access, privacy, sources and authority

Folder: `monitoring/`. Core onboarding.

- **access.tools** (core): Which tools and accounts exist, what read/write scopes are granted, and where are manual alternatives?
  Why: Installed tools and subscriptions do not prove access. File: `monitoring/authority.md`.
- **access.secrets** (core): How will secure invitations and credentials be handled outside this workspace?
  Why: Never ask for passwords, API keys, cookies or tokens in chat or Git. File: `monitoring/authority.md`.
- **privacy.rules** (core): Which data is confidential, restricted, consent-bound or subject to retention and deletion rules?
  Why: Keep client and customer material inside its authorized private boundary. File: `monitoring/authority.md`.
- **authority.actions** (core): What may the assistant do locally, what needs review, and who alone can publish, send, spend or change production?
  Why: Record desired delegation without granting it through the template. File: `monitoring/authority.md`.
- **sources.locations** (core): List the source files, documents, datasets and relevant public pages already available.
  Why: Read supplied context first and ask only for missing information. File: `monitoring/authority.md`.
- **sources.owners** (core): Who maintains each source, how fresh must it be, and what is the evidence of its last successful refresh?
  Why: A scheduled job or file timestamp is not a successful refresh. File: `monitoring/authority.md`.
- **sources.conflicts**: What is the source priority when files disagree, and which unresolved questions need an owner decision?
  Why: Keep conflicts visible instead of choosing the convenient copy. File: `monitoring/authority.md`.
- **authority.regulated**: Which claims, regulated topics, endorsements, licenses, accessibility or legal reviews require a specialist?
  Why: Route actual requirements to an appropriate reviewer; do not invent jurisdictional advice. File: `monitoring/authority.md`.
- **authority.pilot** (core): What one small, reversible task can prove the workspace and handoffs work before live integration?
  Why: Separate setup checks from production reliability. File: `monitoring/authority.md`.
- **authority.handoff** (core): Who maintains this workspace, reviews changes and receives the handover?
  Why: Give maintenance and offboarding an owner. File: `monitoring/authority.md`.
